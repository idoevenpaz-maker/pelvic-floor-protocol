Pelvic Floor Protocol

    The program

    Three phases, each four weeks

    Load rises by changing position (lying, then sitting, then standing and moving), hold time and speed. Effort for holds is about 70% of maximum. Stay in a phase longer if you cannot complete its holds with full, easy release between them.

    The shape of every week

    Day 1Train
    Day 2Train
    Day 3Train
    Day 4Train
    Day 5Train
    Day 6Train
    Day 7Release

    Day 7 is a release session: breathing, lengthening and hip mobility only. Muscles adapt during recovery, and an over-trained pelvic floor tends to stay clenched. After week 12, keep the Control session 3–4 times a week to hold your gains.

    How it works

    The muscles behind the erection valve

    An erection depends on blood flowing in and being trapped. Three pelvic floor muscles help with the trapping and with the ejaculation reflex itself.

    Rigidity

    Ischiocavernosus

    Wraps the roots of the penis. Its contraction squeezes the erectile chambers and pushes pressure inside them above blood pressure, which is what turns a full erection into a rigid one.

    Outflow valve · ejaculation

    Bulbospongiosus

    Surrounds the bulb of the penis and presses on the veins that drain it, slowing outflow. Its rhythmic spasms also drive ejaculation, so learning to keep it calm at high arousal is the core of control.

    Support · tone

    Levator ani

    The hammock beneath the pelvic organs. It gives the other two a stable base and holds resting tone. When it is chronically tight it can lower the ejaculation threshold and cause pelvic pain.

    Technique

    Find the right muscle first

    Locate it

    1. Imagine stopping your urine stream and holding back wind at the same moment. The inward lift you feel at the base of the penis and behind the scrotum is the pelvic floor.
    2. Check in front of a mirror: a correct squeeze draws the base of the penis in and lifts the testicles slightly. The penis may nod upward.
    3. Place two fingers on the perineum, the area between scrotum and anus. You should feel it firm up and lift away from your fingers, then soften when you let go.
    4. Stopping urine mid-stream is only a one-time test to find the muscle. Do not practise on the toilet; it can interfere with normal bladder emptying.

    Common mistakes

    • Holding your breath. Breathe steadily through every hold.
    • Squeezing the buttocks, inner thighs or abdominals instead. Keep them soft; put a hand on your belly to check.
    • Pushing down instead of lifting up during a contraction.
    • Going at 100% on every rep. Holds work best at about 70%; flicks are the only all-out effort.
    • Rushing the release. The relaxation half of each rep is the half that builds control.
    • Adding extra sets. More is not better here; fatigue leaves the floor tense and sore.

    What each exercise does

    Ejaculatory control module · from week 3 · 2–3× per week · 15–20 min

    Arousal training

    Pelvic strength gives you the hardware. Control is a skill learned under real arousal, so it is practised separately, during solo stimulation, outside the daily 10 minutes. It is based on the start-stop method (Semans, 1956), still a first-line behavioural treatment.

    Rate your arousal on a 0–10 scale

    1–5 · building6–7 · work zone8–9 · plateau10

    10 is ejaculation. Just before it sits the point of no return: once the reflex fires, nothing stops it. All the training happens in the 6–8 range, well before that point.

    The key idea: at high arousal, relax the pelvic floor and lengthen your exhale. Most men clench and hold their breath, and that tension feeds the ejaculation reflex. The reverse Kegel and slow breathing from your daily sessions are the tools you use here.

    Weeks 3–6 · Level 1

    Start–stop

    1. Stimulate yourself to 6–7.
    2. Stop completely. Exhale slowly, soften the jaw, belly and pelvic floor.
    3. Wait until arousal drops to 3–4, then resume.
    4. Complete 3–4 cycles before allowing yourself to finish.
    Weeks 7–9 · Level 2

    Slow down, don't stop

    1. Approach 7 as before.
    2. Reduce speed and pressure instead of stopping.
    3. Breathe out through 7–8 and release the floor; let arousal settle by a point or two.
    4. Aim for 15 minutes in the 6–8 range.
    Weeks 10–12 · Level 3

    Ride the plateau

    1. Hold arousal at 7–8 with breath and relaxation alone, without changing stimulation.
    2. If you near 9, use one firm 5–8 second squeeze, then a full release and long exhale.
    3. Carry the same skills into partnered sex, starting with slower positions and an agreed pause signal.
    Safety

    See a doctor first if any of these apply

    Get checked before starting

    • Erection problems that are new or getting worse. They can be the first sign of heart disease, diabetes or low testosterone, often years before other symptoms. Ask for blood pressure, glucose, lipids and testosterone.
    • Pain in the pelvis, perineum, testicles or penis, or pain on ejaculation.
    • Blood in urine or semen, or new difficulty passing urine.
    • Recent prostate surgery or pelvic radiotherapy. Work with a pelvic floor physiotherapist.
    • A new bend or lump in the penis.

    Stop and get assessed if

    • The exercises cause pain, or symptoms get worse after 2–3 weeks. That pattern suggests an overactive floor; switch to release-day sessions only until you are assessed.

    What multiplies the results

    • Aerobic exercise: about 150 minutes a week of brisk walking, cycling or running improves erectile function on its own.
    • Sleep: 7–9 hours. Testosterone and night-time erections both depend on it.
    • No smoking, moderate alcohol, and a healthy waistline. Each affects blood vessels directly.
    • Stress and performance anxiety are major drivers of both early ejaculation and erection loss. The breathing work helps; a sex therapist helps more if anxiety dominates.
    • Medication: this program combines safely with prescribed treatments such as PDE5 inhibitors. Do not stop a prescription without your doctor.
    • One physiotherapy visit with biofeedback is the fastest way to confirm you are contracting the right muscle.

    What to expect

    Most men notice easier contractions in 2–3 weeks. Firmer erections and better control usually appear between weeks 6 and 12. In a randomised trial of pelvic floor training for erectile dysfunction, 40% of men regained normal function and a further 35% improved. In a 12-week rehabilitation study for lifelong premature ejaculation, 61% gained control of the ejaculatory reflex.
